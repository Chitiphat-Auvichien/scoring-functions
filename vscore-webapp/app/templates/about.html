Method, and what these numbers mean

The seven scores

Each normal mode is projected against seven reference motions. All seven come from one displacement vector d and the equilibrium geometry.

s[Tx], s[Ty], s[Tz] mean of the per-atom unit displacements, per axis. Range [−1, 1]; ±1 = every atom moving together along that axis.
s[Rx], s[Ry], s[Rz] for each off-axis atom, r⊥ and d normalised separately, crossed, and the axis component taken — keeping the sin φ factor, so motion that isn't purely tangential is down-weighted. Range [−1, 1].
s[VS] weighted mean of per-bond directional cosines, Σ w|Δb|²|cos| / Σ w|Δb|². Range [0, 1]; 1 = pure bond-length change, 0 = pure angle change. Weight w is the reduced mass μAB under the active mu variant.

The label

Six external slots (three translations, three rotations — two if the molecule is linear) are assigned across the whole mode pool by a one-to-one Hungarian assignment maximising Σ|score|. An assigned mode is "clean" only if it passes both gates: |slot score| ≥ τTR and s[VS] ≤ τB; otherwise it carries a trailing *. Everything unassigned is labelled from s[VS] alone: S stretching (≥ τS), B bending (≤ τB), SB mixed in between.

In practice the six slots are always won by the ideal T/R reference modes, which score exactly 1.000 — across the 26 reference molecules, none of their 463 real vibrations ever took an external slot. So for an uploaded frequency job, expect S/B/SB throughout. The reference rows are shown (toggle "reference modes") as a sanity check on the alignment, not as results.

The highlighted cell

Largest absolute value of the seven, not largest raw value. s[VS] alone cannot be negative, so a raw comparison would favour it structurally — it would win 64% of modes rather than 57%, and would pick +0.087 over −0.283. A mode translating along −x is exactly as translational as one along +x.

Connectivity is never guessed

Bonds come from your input deck or your .vsc, and every result page lists the bonds it used. There is no distance cutoff anywhere in this app. An empty bond list makes s[VS] exactly 0.000 for every mode and labels the whole table B — a silent, plausible, wrong answer — so missing connectivity is an error, not a default.

Frame

The molecule and its displacement vectors are rotated together into the principal axes of inertia before scoring, so x, y, z in the table mean the principal axes. s[VS] is rotation-invariant; the T and R scores are not, and are only meaningful in this frame. The 3D viewer shows that same frame.

Scope

This app runs the scoring algorithm and nothing else. Calibration of τTR/τS/τB, library ingest, PED merging and figure generation all live in the reference implementation. The thresholds shown on each result are read from its published calibration and are not editable here — they are cut points on an s[VS] distribution, so pairing them with a different V-score definition would silently mislabel stretches and bends.

Verification

The scoring core is a verbatim copy of the reference implementation (scoring.py, classifier.py, utils.py, thresholds.json), with only import paths adjusted. All 24 reference molecules with both a log and an input deck reproduce their published results with a worst-case deviation of 0.00e+00 and no label changes, and all 69 log/deck pairs round-trip through a downloaded .vsc with identical scores and labels.
